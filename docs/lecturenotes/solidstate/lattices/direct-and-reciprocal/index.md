# Direct and reciprocal lattice bases

## Direct lattice

A $d$-dimensional direct Bravais lattice is generated by linearly independent
primitive vectors $\mathbf a_1,\ldots,\mathbf a_d$. Collecting these vectors as
columns gives the primitive-basis matrix

$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf a_1&\cdots&\mathbf a_d\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Every direct-lattice vector has the form

$$
\mathbf R_{\mathbf n}=A\mathbf n,
\qquad \mathbf n\in\mathbb Z^d.
$$

The fundamental-region measure is

$$
\Omega=|\det A|.
$$

It is a length in one dimension, an area in two dimensions, and a volume in
three dimensions.

## Reciprocal lattice

The reciprocal primitive-basis matrix is

$$
B=2\pi A^{-\mathsf T}.
$$

Its columns $\mathbf b_1,\ldots,\mathbf b_d$ satisfy

$$
\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf b_j=2\pi\delta_{ij},
$$

or, in matrix form,

$$
A^{\mathsf T}B=2\pi I.
$$

Every reciprocal-lattice vector is

$$
\mathbf G_{\mathbf m}=B\mathbf m,
\qquad \mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^d.
$$

If direct vectors use one consistent length unit, reciprocal vectors use its
inverse. The numerical lattice records do not select that physical unit for the
caller.

## Fundamental-region measures

Taking determinants of the duality relation gives

$$
|\det A|\,|\det B|=(2\pi)^d.
$$

This identity provides a direct numerical check of a constructed reciprocal
basis. It does not identify a crystal structure or a finite sample.

## Physical vectors and finite index domains

`DirectLattice1D`, `DirectLattice2D`, and `DirectLattice3D` represent physical
vector mappings from integer coefficients to Cartesian vectors.
`FinitePeriodicDomain` instead represents a finite quotient of an integer index
lattice. It carries extents and ordering, but no lattice vectors, unit cell, or
physical units. These objects answer different questions and are not
interchangeable.
