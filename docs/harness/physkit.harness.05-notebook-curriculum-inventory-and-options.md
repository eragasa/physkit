# PhysKit Notebook Curriculum Inventory and Options

**Status:** Proposed for human review

## 1. Purpose and decision boundary

This document provides a repository-grounded static inventory of every committed notebook under `notebooks/`, provisional subject and level classifications, maturity and quality observations, alternative organizational models, and decision support for later human selection.

It does **not** establish a final curriculum, an accepted topic sequence, notebook destinations, lifecycle states, canonical artifacts, or migration authorization. The classifications below are inventory judgments, not protected decisions. No notebook was executed, edited, moved, renamed, deleted, or repaired.

The approximate anchors supplied by the human instruction—Young and Freedman, Shankar, physics-major thermal physics near Schroeder’s level, Schroeder, Kittel, Sze and Lee, Callister, and a physics-centered materials synthesis—are used only to estimate breadth, depth, and orientation. No proprietary structure or mandatory sequence is reproduced.

## 10. Hierarchy options

### Option A — Course-oriented hierarchy

**Organizing principle:** group notebooks primarily by broad teaching sequences or course-level collections, while treating the supplied anchors as approximate scope guides rather than chapter templates.

- **Advantages:** familiar entry points for instructors and learners; supports bounded course delivery; can make introductory breadth visible.
- **Disadvantages:** institutional course boundaries vary; shared topics can be duplicated conceptually; advanced and special-topic notebooks fit awkwardly.
- **Cross-listed topics:** require indexes or metadata to show, for example, PIAB in both modern physics and QM, or blackbody radiation in modern physics and statistical mechanics.
- **Computational methods:** risk being repeated inside courses unless a separate shared-method view is maintained.
- **Likely migration complexity:** high, because current subject, scratch, course, and application folders would need many human placement decisions.
- **Premature-commitment risk:** highest; course labels can silently prescribe prerequisites and sequencing.

### Option B — Subject-oriented hierarchy

**Organizing principle:** group notebooks by physics domain and governing model without institutional course numbering.

- **Advantages:** stable across institutions; aligns naturally with upper-level domains and current repository clusters; keeps governing models visible.
- **Disadvantages:** introductory learners lack an obvious route; level mixing can occur inside a subject; cross-domain notebooks remain contested.
- **Cross-listed topics:** handled through secondary-family metadata, indexes, or curriculum maps rather than duplicate files.
- **Computational methods:** may form a shared foundation family, but domain-specific numerical context must remain linked.
- **Likely migration complexity:** medium; many current folders already resemble subjects, but inconsistent names and special-topic locations remain.
- **Premature-commitment risk:** lower than Option A, though subject boundaries can still harden too early.

### Option C — Hybrid hierarchy

**Organizing principle:** present introductory material through broad course-oriented views while organizing upper-level and advanced material primarily by subject and governing model.

- **Advantages:** offers accessible introductory routes without forcing upper-level work into course numbers; accommodates the repository’s mixture of lecture and domain material.
- **Disadvantages:** requires an explicit boundary between introductory and formal subject treatment; may create two navigation logics and additional cross-listing work.
- **Cross-listed topics:** especially important at transition seams such as modern physics/QM, introductory thermal/statistical mechanics, and introductory waves/electromagnetism.
- **Computational methods:** best represented as shared resources visible from both introductory views and upper-level subjects.
- **Likely migration complexity:** medium to high; fewer forced placements than Option A, but more metadata or indexing is needed.
- **Premature-commitment risk:** medium; the hybrid boundary itself becomes a protected human decision.

**Advisory recommendation:** Option B is the least committal basis for a first exact mapping because it follows observable subject/model clusters and does not encode an institutional course sequence. Option C remains a strong alternative if introductory learner navigation is prioritized. This recommendation is decision support only; no option is selected.

No exact directory tree or notebook destination is proposed.

## 11. Boundary questions

- **Physics I, II, and III:** course packaging varies; current notebooks do not justify fixed bundles or numbering.
- **Waves and optics:** Fourier and plane-wave methods connect mechanics, electromagnetism, optics, QM, and solid state; a primary home and cross-listing view remain undecided.
- **Introductory thermal physics:** ideal gas, kinetic theory, blackbody radiation, and elementary distributions could appear in introductory or formal thermal/statistical contexts depending on learning objectives.
- **Modern Physics versus formal QM:** de Broglie, blackbody radiation, PIAB, and basic oscillators span the transition; mathematical depth and operator emphasis should determine later placement.
- **Classical Thermodynamics versus Statistical Mechanics:** mixtures, equations of state, ideal gases, and distributions expose both macroscopic and microscopic views; ownership should follow the governing model rather than title alone.
- **Solid State versus Semiconductor Physics:** lattice and band foundations support semiconductor carrier statistics and transport; foundation, application, and device layers need separate human boundaries.
- **Semiconductor Physics versus device engineering:** junction electrostatics and mobility can support physics instruction or device modeling; the accepted use and depth remain undecided.
- **Materials Physics versus Callister-style Materials Science:** continuum constitutive models, phase fields, interatomic potentials, mechanical-property data, and processing examples vary between physics explanation and engineering application.
- **Shared computational methods:** grids, finite differences, eigensolvers, Monte Carlo, transforms, fitting, and visualization could be centrally indexed, taught in context, or both.
- **Advanced research-derived notebooks:** thin-film, viscoelasticity, semiconductor, and materials-data work may be valuable special topics without belonging to a core sequence.
- **PIAB:** eventual placement depends on the selected organization and explicit modern-physics/QM boundary. PIAB remains parked; this inventory does not resume its planning.

## 12. Cross-listing options

Multiple pedagogical relationships can be represented without copying notebook files. Later options include lightweight metadata with primary and related families; hand-maintained subject or course indexes; prerequisite tables; related-topic links within documentation; curriculum maps; and generated views derived from accepted metadata. Metadata favors machine use, indexes favor transparent review, and generated views reduce duplicated navigation at the cost of tooling and authority questions.

No mechanism is selected or implemented. Any mechanism must preserve one notebook file as the referenced artifact and avoid turning a generated view into a competing authority.
